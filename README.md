# BASTION

**Balanced AI-driven Superconducting Storage and Intelligent Optimization Network**

AI-Assisted Predictive Energy Management for Superconducting Magnetic Energy Storage Systems (SMES)

---

## Overview

BASTION is a personal engineering project developed to integrate MATLAB, Simulink, Simscape, Control Design, and Machine Learning into a single end-to-end workflow.

The system uses a machine learning model to predict future energy demand and utilizes those predictions to influence the charging and discharging behavior of a Superconducting Magnetic Energy Storage (SMES) system.

The objective of the project was not to create a production-ready energy management platform, but to demonstrate the integration of forecasting, control systems, and physical system modeling within the MATLAB ecosystem.

---

## Project Objectives

- Generate synthetic energy demand data
- Train a machine learning model for demand forecasting
- Integrate the predictor within Simulink
- Design a control strategy based on prediction error
- Model an SMES subsystem using Simscape
- Analyze current flow and stored energy behavior
- Demonstrate an end-to-end AI-assisted energy management workflow

## Theory

### Superconductors

A superconductor is a material that exhibits nearly zero electrical resistance when cooled below a critical temperature.

Unlike conventional conductors, superconductors allow electrical current to flow with extremely low energy losses. This property enables the creation of powerful magnetic fields and efficient energy storage systems.

Key characteristics:

- Near-zero electrical resistance
- High current carrying capability
- Strong magnetic field generation
- Extremely high efficiency

---

### Superconducting Magnetic Energy Storage (SMES)

SMES stores electrical energy in the magnetic field generated by a current circulating through a superconducting coil.

Unlike batteries, which store energy chemically, SMES stores energy electromagnetically and can respond almost instantaneously to changes in demand.

The stored energy is given by:

E = 0.5 × L × I²

Where:

- E = Stored Energy (J)
- L = Inductance (H)
- I = Coil Current (A)

Advantages:

- Fast response time
- High efficiency
- Long operational life
- High power density

Challenges:

- Requires cryogenic cooling
- High installation cost
- Complex infrastructure

## Limitations

This project is intended as a proof-of-concept and learning exercise.

Current limitations include:

- Synthetic demand data was used instead of real-world grid data.
- The machine learning model is relatively simple and not optimized for production forecasting.
- The controller is a basic error-driven implementation and has not been extensively tuned.
- The SMES subsystem is a simplified representation and does not include cryogenic system modeling.
- Due to computational limitations associated with running machine learning inference inside Simulink, simulation time was limited to relatively short durations.
- Long-duration performance and grid-scale behavior were not evaluated.
- The project focuses on demonstrating integration rather than maximizing prediction accuracy or control performance.
